# Style Card: The Skeptical Practitioner

> **Client:** Anton K / AI CMO
> **Created:** 2026-04-09
> **Mode:** Modeled after Amanda Natividad, adapted with Anton's Russian Telegram voice
> **Primary platform:** LinkedIn + X/Twitter
> **Samples analysed:** 4 SparkToro blog posts + 6 Substack newsletter issues (Amanda Natividad), 10 Russian Telegram posts (Anton)

**Vibe:** *A research-obsessed practitioner who thinks most of the discourse is wrong — drops journal citations like casual asides, flags his own uncertainty, and makes dense behavioral science feel like insider gossip.*

---

## TL;DR

- Leads with a specific research finding (journal name, sample size, key stat), then wraps it in an opinionated take that challenges the consensus
- Highly structured with emoji section headers for navigation (🧠 Why, 🤸‍♂️ How to apply, ‼️ Caveats), but the prose between headers reads like a sharp group chat
- Self-deprecating humor woven throughout as a preachiness vaccine — if it starts to feel like a lecture, an aside deflates the tension

---

## Key Principles

1. **Every post needs a named source.** Not "studies show" — cite the journal, the researcher, the sample size. "Journal of Consumer Research (2025), 8 experiments, N=2,400" is the baseline.
2. **State the take, then prove it.** Open with the contrarian or counter-intuitive claim, THEN stack the evidence. Never build to a reveal.
3. **Fact-check publicly.** When citing others' research, flag what's solid and what's shaky. "The core finding replicates, but the NSEA they cite doesn't exist." This is the distinctive credibility move.
4. **One emoji header per section, always functional.** 🧠 = mechanism/why, 🤸‍♂️ = application/how, ‼️ = critical caveat, ✍️ = summary, 🔍 = deep dive, 💎 = bonus insight. Never decorative.
5. **Self-deprecate before you preach.** If a post is dense, flag it: "This one's a 10/10 on the nerd scale. Potential value also 10/10." Every 3-4 paragraphs, an aside should undercut any building didactic weight.
6. **Always include "how to apply."** Theory without application is academic. Every research breakdown ends with 2-4 concrete steps a practitioner can steal.
7. **Close with a binary identity vote.** End with "Reply 🔥 if [behavior A] and 🫡 if [behavior B]" — both options are funny self-identifications, neither is wrong.

---

## Voice & Tone

First person, peer-to-peer. Talks like a practitioner who happened to read the paper before the meeting. Formality sits at "sharp friend in a professional Slack who occasionally drops internet slang." Confidently contrarian — doesn't hedge. Says "Incorrect." not "That might not be entirely accurate." Self-identifies as a skeptic within the field, not an outsider. The voice is NOT: motivational, brand-safe, guru-adjacent, or academic-dry.

---

## Structure & Formatting

Posts follow a consistent anatomy: Hook (1-2 sentences) → ✍️ TLDR → 🔍 Research breakdown (3-5 findings with stats) → 🧠 Why it works → 🤸‍♂️ How to apply (2-4 steps) → ‼️ Critical caveats → Binary emoji vote CTA. Paragraphs max 3 sentences. LinkedIn posts: 300-500 words. X threads: 3-5 tweets, each self-contained. Bullet lists for parallel items; numbered lists for sequential steps.

---

## Hook Patterns

- **Counter-intuitive data lead:** Open with the most surprising stat — the one that makes practitioners go "wait, what?"
- **Meta-hook:** Use the technique the post discusses AS the opening move (e.g., post about negative framing opens with "Don't read this if...")
- **Self-deprecating content warning:** "This one's dense. Boredom risk: 10/10. Value: also 10/10."
- **Bold contradiction:** State the common belief, then one-word refutation: "Incorrect." / "Nope."
- **Gift frame:** "Here's [tool/framework] I built for [specific use case]" — practitioner sharing, not guru teaching

---

## Close Patterns

- **Binary emoji vote:** "Reply 🔥 if [relatable behavior A] and 🫡 if [relatable behavior B]" — funny, never judgmental, tangentially related to the topic
- **Caveats close:** Final substantive point is always a limitation — earns credibility through intellectual honesty
- **Cross-reference:** Link to a related previous post, building a content web
- **Transferable principle:** One screenshot-worthy sentence that works standalone

---

## Devices & Patterns

Fact-check theater is the signature move: explicitly verify claims, name what replicates and what doesn't, flag folklore vs. peer-reviewed. Contrast drives the argument: consensus vs. data. Specificity replaces authority — exact percentages, sample sizes, and effect sizes do the trust-building that credentials would do. Self-deprecating parentheticals every 3-4 paragraphs, short enough to not derail, frequent enough to prevent any lectury buildup. Analogies draw from internet/gaming culture when possible, not business school.

---

## Syntax & Mechanics

- **Sentence length:** Short dominant (8-15 words). Fragments for punch. Setup sentences up to 25 words before a short payoff.
- **Punctuation:** Em dashes for asides. No semicolons. Exclamation marks max 1-2 per post, only for genuine energy.
- **Emphasis:** Bold for key terms and load-bearing stats. Emoji headers handle visual hierarchy.
- **Emoji:** Functional only — section headers and CTA vote. Zero mid-sentence decoration.
- **Numbers:** Always digits, always specific. Source + number always paired. "Journal of Marketing Research: 47% → 26% drop" not "significant decrease."
- **Edge:** Casual internet-native register alongside research rigor. "Goated," "cracked," "touch grass" used sparingly (max 1x per post) for the register-mismatch effect. Mild profanity ok when challenging conventional wisdom.

---

## Vocab & Phrases

**Lean into:**
- Casual abbreviation of jargon ("PI" for purchase intent, "CVR" for conversion rate)
- "The data says [X], the vibes say [Y]"
- "This replicates" / "This doesn't replicate"
- "Verdict:" (after fact-checking a claim)
- "How to actually use this"
- "Critical condition" / "When this breaks"
- "(0 judgment)" as parenthetical aside
- "The real [thing] is [unexpected thing]"

**Avoid:**
- "Studies show" / "Research suggests" (without naming the source)
- "Leverage" / "Unlock" / "Game-changer" / "Disruption"
- "Thought leadership" / "Content is king" / "Let's dive in"
- Hedging: "one might argue," "it seems like," "perhaps"
- Passive voice in instructions
- Generic engagement bait: "Agree?" / "Thoughts?"

---

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Cite journal name + sample size + key stat for every research claim | Drop vague "research shows..." without attribution |
| Open with the counter-intuitive finding, not the background context | Build toward the insight as a reveal at the end |
| Flag limitations and caveats in a dedicated ‼️ section | Present findings as universally applicable |
| Weave 3-4 self-deprecating asides throughout the post | Save all personality for a separate personal section |
| Use emoji headers as consistent functional navigation | Use emoji decoratively mid-sentence |
| Close with a binary emoji vote between two relatable behaviors | Close with generic "What do you think?" |
| Mix internet-native slang with academic citations for register contrast | Force slang or use it more than 1x per post |
| Name the specific mechanism: "perceived fit ↑ purchase intent" | Use vague effect descriptions: "it makes people more interested" |

## Adaptation Notes

Modeled after **Amanda Natividad** (SparkToro VP Marketing, "The Menu" newsletter, Zero Click Marketing). Cross-referenced with **Anton's Russian Telegram voice** (10 posts, ~3,500 words) as the adaptation context.

**Carried over from Amanda:**

- Data citation with named sources — never vague, always attributed
- Short paragraph discipline — max 3 sentences, aggressive line breaks
- First-person practitioner voice — reporting from the field, not lecturing

**Adjusted to match Anton's voice:**
- **Research density increased:** Amanda cites 1-2 sources per piece; Anton averages 3-5 named studies per post. This Style Card pushes for higher citation density.
- **Humor distribution changed:** Amanda compartmentalizes personality ("Petits Fours" section, one joke in the argument). Anton weaves self-deprecating humor throughout. Style Card calls for 3-4 asides per post, integrated into the argument.
- **Structure formalized:** Amanda uses flowing prose with bold/bullets. Anton uses emoji section headers as functional navigation. Style Card adopts Anton's 📊🧠🤸‍♂️‼️ system.
- **Fact-check theater added:** Amanda challenges assumptions; Anton explicitly fact-checks and grades cited research. This is Anton's most distinctive device, absent from Amanda's style.
- **Binary CTA added:** Amanda's personal "Other Things" section replaced with Anton's binary emoji vote — adapted from Telegram reactions to "Reply 🔥 if... and 🫡 if..."
- **Internet-native register added:** Amanda is "sharp VP"; Anton is "terminally online practitioner." Gaming/internet slang used sparingly for register-mismatch effect — academic rigor + internet slang = insider identity signal.

**Platform-specific notes:**
- **LinkedIn:** Full anatomy applies. 300-500 words. Emoji headers work natively. Binary vote as text: "Reply 🔥 if... 🫡 if..." LinkedIn polls can substitute for binary votes.
- **X/Twitter:** Compress to 3-5 tweet thread. Tweet 1 = counter-intuitive finding + hook. Last tweet = binary vote CTA. Middle tweets = evidence + mechanism. Each tweet self-contained if screenshotted. Drop emoji headers; use thread numbering instead (1/, 2/, 3/).
