## 1. Discovery

*How they find us*

**Peer recommendation and Slack DMs** — The dominant discovery channel. First marketing hires operate in tight peer networks: other first hires at startups one stage ahead, former colleagues who recently took similar roles, and small Slack groups for growth practitioners. When they ask "what tools are you using to build your growth strategy?" in a private Slack channel, that is the highest-trust discovery moment. They trust people who have recently been in their exact situation, not brands or influencers. Research confirms solo marketers rely on "blogs, podcasts, and conferences for guidance, as solo hires lack internal mentors" (Marketri, 2026).

**LinkedIn content from practitioners** — Second-highest discovery channel. They consume LinkedIn daily (Tuesday-Thursday morning and lunch are peak engagement times per audience profile). They follow growth operators at companies one stage ahead. Discovery happens when a practitioner posts a first-person account ("How I built a growth strategy in my first 30 days as the only marketer") and mentions Diffmode in the narrative. They respond to specificity: stage, team size, budget, and timeline must all be present. Generic "AI marketing tool" posts get scrolled past.

**Newsletter references (Lenny's, Demand Curve, Forget The Funnel)** — Third channel. Lenny's Newsletter (500K+ subscribers per Perplexity, 2026) is the "growth bible" for this audience. Demand Curve's growth program and newsletter are canonical references. Forget The Funnel's "Your First 90 Days as a Marketer in SaaS" by Claire Suellentrop and Gia Laudi is widely cited. A mention or case study in any of these newsletters would be a high-trust discovery vector. Currently Diffmode has no presence in these channels. [INFERENCE: newsletter sponsorship or earned mention would be high-impact based on audience consumption patterns, not direct evidence of current discovery.]

**Reddit lurking (r/startups, r/SaaS, r/marketing)** — Fourth channel. This audience lurks more than they post. They search for threads like "first marketing hire strategy" or "growth strategy tools for startups." Reddit discussions confirm that early-stage marketers compare tools against the DIY stack and seek unfiltered peer opinions (Perplexity research, 2026). No specific Diffmode-related threads exist yet. Perplexity research found limited direct community discussions about AI growth strategy platforms, indicating the category is still nascent in community discourse.

**Google Search (comparison and problem terms)** — Fifth channel. Searches like "how to build a growth strategy for my startup," "Demand Curve alternative," "growth strategy template seed stage," and "AI marketing strategy tool" drive traffic. Diffmode does not yet rank for these terms. Competitors like Averi.ai and FounderPal appear in "best AI marketing tools for startups" listicles (Averi.ai blog, ConvertMate, 2026).

**What triggers first awareness:**
Two primary paths: (1) **Active search triggered by CEO pressure.** The CEO asks "What's our top acquisition channel going to be, and why?" before a board meeting. The first hire has opinions but no systematic analysis. They search for a framework or tool that can compress weeks of research into days. This is the highest-intent discovery moment. (2) **Passive exposure via peer content.** They see a LinkedIn post from another first hire who documented their strategy-building process, mentioning a tool that helped. The emotional driver is "that person is in my exact situation and found a way through." The emotional context behind these moments lives in `content-audience-profile-first-marketing-hire.md` (Section 5: Emotional Register).

## 2. Evaluation

*What they ask, compare, and object to*

**Questions they ask during evaluation:**
- "Will this give me something I can actually present to my CEO, or is it just another list of generic ideas?"
- "How is this different from what I'd get from ChatGPT if I spent 3 hours prompting it?"
- "Does it work for MY specific product and stage, or is it one-size-fits-all startup advice?"
- "Can I see what the actual output looks like before I commit?"
- "How long does it take to get results? I don't have weeks."
- "Is this backed by real data, or is it AI-generated marketing advice dressed up as strategy?"
- "Will my CEO take this seriously, or will it look like I outsourced my job to a tool?"
- "What happens if the recommendations don't work? Do I get support or am I on my own?"

**Comparisons they make:**

**Diffmode vs. "Google Sheets + ChatGPT + my network"** — This is the real competitor, not a named tool. The DIY stack is free, flexible, and familiar. The first hire already has a Notion doc with 47 bookmarked articles, a half-finished competitor spreadsheet, and ChatGPT conversations saved. The DIY approach works for initial hypothesis testing but breaks down at: (a) no memory or context across sessions, (b) no systematic competitive analysis, (c) no way to prioritize tactics against specific constraints, and (d) outputs aren't presentation-ready. Research confirms: "ChatGPT resets per session, leading to inconsistent voice and hours reformatting prompts" and "manual overload burns 10-20 hours/week" (Perplexity research, 2026). The transition point is when they realize the DIY approach can't produce something defensible for a board meeting.

**Diffmode vs. hiring a growth consultant ($200-$500/hr)** — The comparison they make when they have budget but not time. A consultant is expensive, slow (weeks of briefing and deliverables), and doesn't have product context. The consultant comparison actually helps Diffmode: "Hours, not weeks" and "$299/mo vs. $5,000+ for a consultant engagement" is a concrete value prop. Per JTBD analysis: "Hiring a growth consultant — expensive and slow; consultant doesn't have product context; requires extensive briefing."

**Diffmode vs. Demand Curve Growth Program ($1,200)** — The comparison they make when considering education vs. output. Demand Curve is the highest-prestige option but requires "1-2 hours daily for the bootcamp" and months of learning before producing output (competitors analysis, 2026). This comparison favors Diffmode when the buyer needs output NOW, not education over months.

**Diffmode vs. "what worked at my last company"** — Not a tool comparison but the real competing behavior. Research confirms: "Relying on what worked at previous company — context-transfer is unreliable; startup's product, ICP, and stage are different" (JTBD analysis). This is the approach they default to under time pressure, and it's the one most likely to fail silently.

**Diffmode vs. FounderPal** — Relevant only for buyers who overlap with the founder segment. FounderPal targets solopreneurs and indie hackers with one-time payment AI marketing tools. First hires may encounter it but dismiss it as "too lightweight" for their needs: "Limited customization... there's not a ton of nuance in how deep you can go" (Reddit user, per competitors analysis). FounderPal generates ideas but not board-ready strategy documents.

**Objections that surface:**

"How is this different from ChatGPT?" *The most common objection. They're AI-literate enough to know most "AI marketing tools" are thin wrappers around ChatGPT (audience profile, anti-triggers). This objection requires a concrete demonstration: show the 616-vector database, the competitive analysis, the specific prioritization logic. Abstract claims about "proprietary AI" trigger the vendor-speak alarm.*

"My CEO will think I'm outsourcing my strategy to a robot." *The credibility objection. They need to feel they're using a research tool, not delegating their job. Positioning must frame Diffmode as "research infrastructure" (like Ahrefs or G2) rather than "AI that does your job." The parallel: nobody questions a marketer for using Ahrefs data to inform SEO strategy.*

"I don't have time to learn another tool right now." *Time scarcity objection. They're already drowning (doing "70-80% of marketing execution" solo per audience profile). Any new tool must deliver value within minutes, not days. The 15-minute growth audit framing directly addresses this, but they need to believe it.*

"$299/month is a lot when I haven't proven this role works yet." *Budget-meets-credibility objection. They have $5K-$20K/month budget but spending it on an unproven tool feels risky when they're still proving their own value. The free tier + sample output reduces this friction, but the real unlock is making the output look like THEIR strategic work, not a tool's output.* [INFERENCE: this objection is extrapolated from JTBD analysis price sensitivity patterns, not directly observed in reviews.]

"I need to see it work for a company like mine before I trust it." *The specificity objection. Fortune 500 case studies are irrelevant (audience profile). They need to see output from a company at their stage: 5-15 employees, seed-funded, specific vertical. The demo report on the landing page (Visibility Pro) partially addresses this, but uses a GEO audit company rather than a SaaS company, which may feel distant.* [INFERENCE: based on audience profile Section 8 proof types.]

## 3. Conversion Triggers

*What closes the deal*

**Seeing a real output sample that matches their situation** — The single most important conversion trigger. When they see an actual strategy document that looks board-ready -- with competitive analysis, specific tactical recommendations, day-by-day execution plans, and budget allocations calibrated to their stage -- the "I could present this" moment collapses most objections. This is analogous to Notion's template conversion path: "Duplicating a template and seeing it work immediately is the fastest convert path." The Diffmode landing page partially delivers this via the demo report, but the demo is for a GEO audit product, not a SaaS company matching the H3 buyer's context.

**The board meeting deadline** — The acute trigger. When the CEO says "present our growth strategy at the next board meeting" and the first hire has 2 weeks, the urgency overrides price objections. At this moment, the value proposition shifts from "nice tool" to "this saves my credibility." The "Hours, not weeks" positioning on the landing page maps directly to this trigger. Per JTBD analysis: "CEO asks in a board prep meeting: 'What's our top acquisition channel going to be, and why?'"

**A peer showing their Diffmode output** — The social proof conversion. When another first hire at a comparable startup shares that they used Diffmode to build their strategy, and the output looks professional and specific, trust transfers immediately. This audience trusts "practitioners who were recently in their exact position" above all other proof types (audience profile). Currently this trigger doesn't exist because there are no customer stories yet. [INFERENCE: this is a projected trigger based on audience trust patterns.]

**The ChatGPT frustration breaking point** — The DIY-to-tool transition trigger. After spending 3+ hours prompting ChatGPT and producing output that feels generic and unpresentable, they search for something better. Research confirms the break point: "No memory/context -- ChatGPT resets per session, leading to inconsistent voice and hours reformatting prompts" (Perplexity research, 2026). Timing: this frustration typically peaks 3-6 weeks into the role, when the board meeting is approaching and the DIY stack hasn't produced a coherent strategy.

**Proof points that close (ranked):**

1. **A sample output from a company at their stage** — A strategy document generated for a seed-stage SaaS with 5-15 employees, specific vertical, and realistic budget. Must include competitive analysis, tactical recommendations, and day-by-day plans. This is the #1 proof point because this audience evaluates by examining deliverables, not reading feature lists: "Output samples they can evaluate themselves -- 'here's what the actual deliverable looks like' beats any description of it" (audience profile).

2. **A first-person account from another first marketing hire** — Someone who was recently in their exact role, used Diffmode, and shares the unfiltered experience including what worked and what didn't. Must include specific metrics: stage, team size, timeline, and dollars. "First-person accounts from someone who was recently the first marketing hire and shares the unfiltered reality -- including what failed" (audience profile).

3. **The time compression math** — "3 weeks of research compressed into 15 minutes" is the landing page claim. If this is credible (backed by a walkthrough or demo), it's powerful because time is the scarcest resource. The comparison: "A strategy consultant charges $5,000+ and takes 3 weeks. Diffmode costs $299/month and takes an afternoon."

4. **The 616-vector database as evidence of rigor** — The specific number (616 proven growth tactics from real companies) signals systematic methodology, not generic AI generation. This addresses the "how is this different from ChatGPT" objection directly. It works because this audience "respects practitioner data, not enterprise research framing" (audience profile) -- the database positions Diffmode as a research tool with proprietary data, not an AI wrapper.

5. **The founder's credibility story** — "Ex-growth lead at JetBrains Academy" and "I built Diffmode and ran it on itself" (from the landing page). This matters because the audience trusts practitioners who have done the work, not consultants who advise on it. The self-referential proof ("the demo shows the actual output, not a mockup") is compelling.

## 4. Stall & Churn Points

*Where they get stuck or leave*

### Stall Points

**"I need to see what I'm getting before I enter my card"** — The #1 stall point. They land on the site, read the copy, see the demo report, but don't start the growth audit because they want to see what THEIR specific output would look like, not a demo for someone else's company. The demo report (Visibility Pro for a GEO audit product) is specific but not THEIR situation. SaaS onboarding data confirms: "84% abandonment in first session" when there's no personalized guidance (Foundey, 2026), and "75% of new users lost in the first week without proper onboarding" (Reprise, 2026). The gap: the free tier offers 1 growth audit, but the buyer needs confidence that the output will be worth 15 minutes of input before starting.

**Growth audit questionnaire abandonment** — They start the 15-minute growth audit but stall partway through because questions require specifics they don't have (exact MRR, specific competitive data, detailed ICP definition). Remember: they were hired because the company DOESN'T have these documented. Per pain point P1: "No strategic foundation -- pushed into growth tactics before positioning/ICP is defined." If the audit assumes they have answers they don't, they bail. [INFERENCE: based on audience profile pain points and general SaaS onboarding abandonment patterns, not direct observation of Diffmode's audit flow.]

**"Looks interesting, I'll come back to this" bookmark-and-forget** — They encounter Diffmode via a LinkedIn post or newsletter reference, bookmark it or save the post, and intend to explore it "when I have time." They never come back because the next 48 hours fill with execution demands. This is the passive stall -- they're interested but never activated. Research confirms: "Without guided tours, pop-ups, or clear paths, users get lost" (Reprise, 2026). The fix is interception at the right frustration moment (see Section 3: "The board meeting deadline" trigger).

**"Let me run this by my CEO first"** — Unlike the solo founder who decides alone, the first marketing hire often needs implicit or explicit approval to use a tool. They don't have procurement authority for $299/month. The stall happens in the gap between personal interest and organizational buy-in. If the CEO asks "what is this?" and the first hire can't articulate the value in 30 seconds, it dies. This is compounded by the credibility objection: they don't want the CEO to think they outsourced strategy to AI.

**Output arrived but feels too generic to present** — They complete the growth audit, receive the output, but the recommendations feel like they could apply to any SaaS startup. If the output doesn't reference THEIR specific competitive landscape, THEIR specific constraints, and THEIR specific stage, they don't convert to paid because the output doesn't pass their "would I put my name on this in front of my CEO?" test. This is the highest-stakes stall for retention: they used the free tier, got underwhelmed, and won't return.

### Churn Reasons

**"I already built my strategy another way"** — The #1 churn driver. The 90-day window is finite. If they find Diffmode after they've already cobbled together a strategy from ChatGPT + Lenny's + their network, the job is done. The tool is useful once (strategy building) but doesn't have ongoing daily utility like an analytics tool. If they don't encounter Diffmode in weeks 2-6 of their role, the acute need has passed. Timing is everything for this segment.

**"The output didn't feel specific enough to justify the price"** — They tried the free tier, the recommendations felt like polished versions of what ChatGPT would produce, and the gap between free AI and $299/month didn't feel justified. This is the category-level churn problem: "AI writing/content yields bland results without customization" (Perplexity research, 2026). For Diffmode, specificity IS the product -- if it doesn't feel dramatically more specific than ChatGPT, the value proposition collapses.

**"My situation changed -- I got more resources or I got fired"** — Context-driven churn. Either the startup raised Series A and hired a VP Marketing (making the first hire's solo-operator constraint irrelevant), or the first hire didn't survive the 90-day window and left the role. Both remove the buyer from the segment entirely. This is structural, not fixable by product improvements.

**"I used it once and got what I needed"** — The one-and-done churn pattern. They bought one month, generated their growth strategy, presented it to the board, and cancelled. The strategy document is a deliverable, not a workflow -- it doesn't require daily interaction. Unless there's a compelling reason to come back (quarterly strategy refresh, new tactic generation, performance tracking), they extract value in month one and leave. [INFERENCE: this is a projected churn pattern based on the JTBD frequency analysis ("Acute once; recurring quarterly thereafter for strategy refreshes") and general SaaS one-time-use patterns.]

## 5. Expansion

*Upgrades & referrals*

**Free -> Diffmode Pro ($299/month)** — The primary upgrade path. Trigger: they complete the free growth audit, receive 1 of 10 tactics, and the visible quality makes the other 9 feel irresistible. The "9 more tactics locked" framing on the demo report is a classic limit-based upgrade trigger. The buyer's calculation: "If this one tactic saves me 5 hours of research, the other 9 are worth $299." The emotional trigger is FOMO -- they can see the section headers of locked content and imagine what they're missing. The landing page's "14-day 'Better Than DIY' Guarantee" reduces conversion risk. Most likely to convert within 24 hours of seeing the free output, when the board meeting pressure is still acute.

**Diffmode Pro -> Quarterly/Annual retention** — The expansion trigger for sustained use is the strategy refresh cycle. Per JTBD: "Recurring quarterly thereafter for strategy refreshes." If Diffmode can position itself as the tool they return to every quarter when the CEO asks "what's working and what should we try next?", retention extends beyond the initial 90-day sprint. This requires the product to offer value beyond the initial strategy generation: competitive landscape updates, tactic performance tracking, or new vector recommendations based on changing constraints.

**Individual -> Team recommendation** — When the first marketing hire succeeds (survives the 90-day window, gets board approval for strategy, starts executing), they become an internal champion. If the startup grows and hires additional marketers, the first hire may recommend Diffmode as "how we built our growth strategy." This is a slow expansion path (6-12 months) but high-value because it comes with internal credibility. [INFERENCE: projected based on organizational growth patterns, not direct evidence.]

**What triggers referrals:**
The referral moment happens in peer conversations -- Slack DMs, coffee chats with other first hires, LinkedIn comments. The specific trigger: someone posts about struggling to build a growth strategy ("Any tools you'd recommend for building a marketing strategy from scratch?") and a Diffmode user replies with their experience. Referrals are word-of-mouth, not viral -- this audience shares selectively and only when they have genuine conviction. The strongest referral trigger is when the referring person can say "I used this for my board presentation and my CEO loved it." The weakest trigger is feature-based: "it has 616 growth tactics" doesn't spread unless the person has personally experienced the output quality.

Second referral trigger: the first hire shares their Diffmode output with a peer for feedback ("Hey, does this strategy make sense for a B2B SaaS at our stage?") and the peer sees the output quality and asks "What tool made this?" This is the organic, output-driven referral path -- the deliverable IS the marketing.
