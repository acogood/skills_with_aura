# Style Card Template

This document defines the exact output format for `writing-style-card.md`. Follow this structure for every style card regardless of which mode generated it.

## Template Structure

```markdown
# Style Card: [Style Name]

> **Client:** [Company name]
> **Created:** [Date]
> **Mode:** [Captured from existing content / Designed from audience profile / Modeled after [Creator Name]]
> **Primary platform:** [LinkedIn / Newsletter / X / Other]
> **Samples analysed:** [X blog posts, X LinkedIn posts, X tweets, etc.] (Modes 1 & 3 only)

**Vibe:** [One-line description of the overall feel — e.g., "Smart peer explaining something over coffee — direct, no hedging, occasional dry humour."]

---

## TL;DR

- [Bullet 1 — the most distinctive aspect of the style]
- [Bullet 2 — the structural signature]
- [Bullet 3 — the tonal signature]

These three bullets should be enough for someone to get the essence of the style without reading the full card.

---

## Key Principles

5-7 specific, testable rules that define this style. Each rule should be concrete enough that you could check a draft against it and say "yes this follows the rules" or "no this breaks rule 3."

- [Rule 1 — e.g., "Open every post with a single concrete observation, not a question or abstract claim"]
- [Rule 2 — e.g., "Never use more than 3 sentences in a paragraph"]
- [Rule 3 — e.g., "Use specific numbers instead of vague qualifiers — '43% of teams' not 'many teams'"]
- [Rule 4]
- [Rule 5]
- [Rule 6 — optional]
- [Rule 7 — optional]

---

## Voice & Tone

[2-4 sentences covering POV, cadence, attitude, emotional range.]

Be specific. Not "confident tone" but "writes like a practitioner sharing hard-won lessons — direct, opinionated, but never preachy. Uses first person. Shifts between sharp observation and dry self-deprecation."

Include:
- Point of view (first person / second person / collective "we")
- Formality level (with a concrete anchor — not "professional" but "professional enough for a VP audience, casual enough to use contractions and the occasional 'look,'")
- Energy level and emotional range
- What the voice is NOT (sometimes defining the negative is clearer)

---

## Structure & Formatting

[2-4 sentences covering how content is built.]

Include measurable norms:
- Typical paragraph length (e.g., "1-3 sentences, never more than 4")
- Line break habits (e.g., "line break after every 1-2 sentences on LinkedIn; longer paragraphs in newsletters")
- Use of bullets vs. flowing prose
- Use of headers/sections
- Scannability — designed to be skimmed or read deeply?
- Typical post/piece length

- **[Pattern name]:** [1-sentence description — e.g., "Reframe question: Ends with a question that reframes the topic, inviting the reader to see it differently."]
- **[Pattern name]:** [description]

- **[Pattern name]:** [description]

## Devices & Patterns

[2-4 sentences on rhetorical moves that define this voice.]

Focus on the moves that make the style recognisable:
- Use of contrast/juxtaposition (before/after, old way/new way)
- Use of questions (rhetorical, direct, Socratic)
- Data and specificity habits (do they cite numbers? how precise?)
- Personal anecdote usage (frequent, rare, only as openers?)
- Analogy and metaphor habits (common domains they draw from?)
- Repetition and rhythm (parallel structure, callbacks, rule of three?)

## Syntax & Mechanics

- **Sentence length:** [e.g., "Averages ≤15 words. Mixes 3-word fragments with occasional 25-word setups for rhythm."]
- **Punctuation:** [e.g., "Heavy use of em dashes for asides. No semicolons. Rare exclamation marks — max one per piece."]
- **Emphasis:** [e.g., "Bold for key phrases only, never full sentences. No ALL CAPS. Italics for titles and internal monologue."]
- **Emoji:** [e.g., "None" or "1-2 per post, only as visual markers for lists"]
- **Numbers:** [e.g., "Always digits, never spelled out. Percentages frequent. Specific > round numbers."]
- **Edge:** [e.g., "Clean language, no profanity. Occasional sharp phrasing when challenging conventional wisdom."]

## Quality Rules

Apply to every Style Card regardless of mode:

1. **Be specific, not vague.** "Writes with energy" is useless. "Opens 80% of posts with a single-sentence bold claim, followed by a line break" is useful. Every rule should be testable.

2. **Abstract patterns, never copy words.** The Style Card describes how to write, not what to write. No sentences lifted from the samples (Modes 1 & 3). No filler phrases (Mode 2).

3. **Prioritise what's distinctive.** Every writer uses paragraphs. Not every writer opens with a one-word sentence followed by an em dash. Focus on the patterns that make this voice recognisable and different from generic content.

4. **Note variations honestly.** If the style shifts between contexts (blog vs LinkedIn) or moods (teaching vs opinionating), say so. A Style Card that pretends the creator is perfectly consistent will produce rigid, unnatural content.

5. **Keep it dense.** Target 500-900 words for the full Style Card. Dense and scannable, not padded. If a section adds no distinctive information, cut it short.

6. **Align with the audience.** If an audience profile exists, the Style Card should be consistent with it. The vocabulary, formality, and tone should match what the audience responds to. If there's a conflict (the creator's natural style clashes with the audience's preferences), note it explicitly.

---

## Mode-Specific Guidance

### Mode 1: Capture My Style

The analysis should surface what makes this creator's voice distinctive. Generic observations ("uses a conversational tone") are wasted space. Look for the specific, unexpected patterns — the quirky punctuation habit, the recurring structural move, the unusual vocabulary choice.

If the samples reveal weak or inconsistent patterns, be honest about it in the delivery summary. A Style Card built on mediocre content will produce mediocre content. Suggest the client consider Mode 2 or 3.

### Mode 2: Design a Style For Me

Every style choice should be deliberate and traceable to something in the audience profile or brand context. Don't generate a generic "professional but friendly" style — design something with a point of view.

The style should feel like it was designed by a senior content strategist who deeply understands this specific audience. It should have opinions — "always lead with data" or "never use questions as hooks" — not just safe middle-ground advice.

Think of this as designing a character: what's their attitude? Their verbal tics? Their go-to moves? What would make someone read two posts and recognise the voice?

### Mode 3: Model After a Creator

The adaptation step is what makes this mode valuable rather than just copycat. The goal is to capture what makes the target creator effective — their structural patterns, their rhythm, their rhetorical moves — while tuning the vocabulary, formality, and references to fit the client's audience.

The Adaptation Notes section should be transparent about what was kept and what was changed. This helps the client understand the thinking and gives them a basis for requesting adjustments.
